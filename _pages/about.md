I am Timur Cinay, a Ph.D. candidate in the MIT Department of Earth, Atmospheric, and Planetary Sciences, advised by Dr. Andrew Babbin. My research focuses on quantifying marine emissions of nitrous oxide and other greenhouse gases, with particular emphasis on high-frequency atmospheric observations and data-driven approaches to improve emission estimates.

Since beginning my Ph.D. in 2021, I have been developing a state-of-the-art atmospheric monitoring station in the Galápagos Islands to measure marine N₂O fluxes at high temporal resolution. More broadly, I am interested in creating and analyzing high-frequency datasets and applying data assimilation techniques to better quantify greenhouse gas emissions and understand how marine systems respond to climate variability, such as El Niño.

In addition to atmospheric measurements, I have been involved in ship-based sampling campaigns focused on surface-ocean greenhouse gas concentrations, coastal upwelling systems, and their role in the global N₂O budget. My work also incorporates high-resolution measurements of inorganic carbon and nitrogen species to estimate nitrogen cycling rates within oxygen-deficient zones.

Overall, my research sits at the ocean–atmosphere interface and is motivated by the goal of constraining greenhouse gas fluxes using robust, data-driven methods.

I earned my B.S. degrees in Chemistry and Environmental Science from the University of Rochester in 2021. I am originally from Keşan, Edirne, Türkiye. Outside of research, I enjoy pottery, gardening, and embroidery.
